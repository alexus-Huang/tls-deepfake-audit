# Results: first measurement (2026-11-08)

**Setup:** the first 317 names of the Tranco list were read and 17 excluded by my own exclusion list, leaving 300 scanned, from campus ResWiFi. SSLyze via the runner: 5 hosts at once, 2 connections per host, 3 s timeout, 1 attempt, certificate + TLS 1.3 + TLS 1.2 cipher suites. Three chunks of 100. Run folders: 20261108-191843_top300-part1, 20261108-192129_top300-part2, 20261108-192454_top300-part3.

## Measurement integrity

- jsdelivr.net returned a certificate issued by "Norton Web/Mail Shield Root... generated by Norton Antivirus for SSL/TLS scanning" with 10,957 days of validity. Antivirus software that inspects HTTPS generates such certificates, so this record most likely reflects a local interceptor and not the server. It is the only Norton-issued record among the 204 completed hosts (0.5%). Its cipher lists (including its empty TLS 1.3 list) are not valid measurements of jsdelivr.net, and the counts above include it. Removing it would change the tables by at most one host. The other 203 records show public CAs, so I have no evidence that they were intercepted.

## Run time

468 s for 300 domains = 1.56 s per host, about 43 hours per 100k hosts at this mix (about 64 hours if every host completed). Timeout tests at 3, 4, and 5 s gave identical outcomes. Run times in the TLS 1.3 timeout test varied up to 6x for the same domains (about 60 s vs 11 s), and in the slow runs no results appeared for about 55 s. Cause not confirmed.

## Observations

- TLS 1.3 is nearly uniform: three suites are offered by over 90% of hosts that support it. 23 of 204 hosts did not accept any TLS 1.3 suite.
- TLS 1.2 is where hosts differ. More than 90% accept the modern forward-secret GCM suites, yet 55% also accept static-RSA suites, 76% accept CBC suites, and 19% still accept 3DES.
- The first 20 hosts showed 7 hosts accepting 3DES from 2 operators, which is why host counts overstate independence.
- Certificate lifetimes range widely (29 to 397 days), so a short lifetime is not unusual for popular hosts. Seven operators issue about 90% of the certificates.

## Caveats

- Hosts are not independent. CDN or operator configurations are shared, so host counts overstate how many separate operators there are.
- "Accepts" is not "prefers." Legacy suites often stay enabled for old clients.
- A top-300 popularity sample is not representative of all internet services. 17 names were excluded by my own choice.
- TLS 1.2 and 1.3 only. Older protocols were not tested.
- DNS failures depend on the resolver used. Connection errors have no recorded reason.
- A local TLS interceptor (Norton) affected at least one record.
